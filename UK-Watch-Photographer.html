A Specialist in Reflective Product Photography

Photographer - Jack Pickerill

Luxury watches are among the more technically demanding products to photograph.

Polished stainless steel, brushed metal, sapphire crystal, precious metals and highly reflective surfaces can all behave very differently under studio lighting. Rather than simply illuminating the watch, the lighting has to be carefully constructed so that the reflections across the case, bracelet and crystal describe the shape and finish of the watch.

Jack's background photographing technically difficult chrome and high-gloss products for Russell Hobbs developed this approach over several years of full-time studio photography. Working with reflective products every day meant developing a detailed understanding of how to control reflections, shape highlights and maintain accurate representation of materials.

That experience translates particularly well to luxury watch photography, where the quality of the lighting can have a significant effect on how premium a watch appears in the finished image.

Lighting Luxury Watches

Lighting is one of the most important parts of luxury watch photography.

A watch made from polished steel or precious metal can reflect almost everything around it. The photographer therefore isn't simply lighting the watch; they are effectively designing the reflections that the watch will reproduce.

Large white reflectors can be positioned around the watch to create controlled highlights across polished metal, while carefully placed black areas can help define edges and shape. The position and size of each light source can dramatically change how the case, bracelet and crystal appear.

This is particularly important when photographing watches with highly polished cases or bracelets, where uncontrolled reflections can make an otherwise premium product look flat, dark or untidy.

Attention to the Details

There are a number of small details that make a significant difference to professional watch photography.

Watch hands are traditionally positioned around 10:10, allowing the brand logo to remain clearly visible between the hands. For macro photography, focus stacking allows fine details across the watch to remain sharp, while reflective surfaces require carefully positioned flags and reflectors.

Dust, fingerprints and small imperfections can then be removed during the retouching process without losing the character and material qualities of the watch.

These details may not be immediately noticeable in the finished photograph, but together they make the difference between an image that simply records a watch and one that presents it as a luxury product.
